Charleston SC · Barnegat Bay NJ · Verra SDVM003

What would a resilience credit have to sell for?

For each restoration site, set what restoring a hectare of marsh costs. The price slider moves to the credit price at which those sales cover the cost. You can also drag the price to see the cost a given price would cover.

One credit is USD 1 of expected annual flood damage to buildings avoided by the restored marsh, counted as in Verra SDVM003. Damage counts where buildings flood more than 10 cm deep and their depth changes by at least 2 cm.

Cost is paid in year 0. The marsh earns credits only once it is 5 years old, so credits start in year 6.

How the price is worked out

EAB_ha = (EAD_baseline − EAD_project) / project area USD per ha per year (Verra Eq. 7–8) credits = EAB_ha per ha in every year t = 6 … N (Eq. 9–10) price = cost per ha / ( EAB_ha × Σ_{t=6..N} (1 + r)^−t ) N = crediting period, r = discount rate
  • Expected annual damage (EAD) comes from the SFINCS runs (Charleston V0005, one model per site; Barnegat Bay V0018, one model of the bay with all six sites raised together) for return periods of 1 to 100 years, with Hazus depth–damage curves applied to each building's depth above its first floor. The baseline is today's marsh.
  • Project area is the set of cells the scenario raises. Verra divides by the change in vegetated area, and here every raised cell is assumed to become vegetated marsh.
  • Not included: Verra's uncertainty deduction, monitoring and verification fees, maintenance after construction, and sea-level rise over the crediting period. Each of these would raise the break-even price.
  • Crediting period is how long credits are sold, and so how long the sales have to cover the cost. Verra SDVM003 (section 1.3) lets the project choose 10 to 50 years; the benefits of the marsh can last longer.
  • Scenarios that do not lower expected damage under this rule earn no credits. They are listed at the top of each site panel and left out of its menu and chart.

Where the cost range comes from

  • Bayraktarov et al. (2016), The cost and feasibility of marine coastal restoration, Ecological Applications 26:1055–1074. For salt marshes (73 projects, all in developed countries), the median restoration cost was US$67,128 per ha. The median total cost, which includes operating costs, was US$151,129 per ha (40 projects). Both are in 2010 dollars, about $101k and $227k in 2026 dollars (CPI factor about 1.5); both are marked on the cost slider, and $227k is the starting cost. doi:10.1890/15-1077
  • Chesapeake Bay Program, Financing and funding tidal wetland restoration in the Chesapeake Bay. Its analysis uses US$30,000–120,000 per acre ($74k–297k per ha), marked as the brown band on the cost slider. chesapeakebay.net
  • US Army Corps of Engineers, marsh creation with dredged material for whooping crane habitat (Gulf Coast). The estimated average cost was US$54,000 per acre ($133k per ha). restorethegulf.gov

Costs vary a lot with how the marsh is raised (thin-layer sediment placement, fill, grading and planting), with land and permitting costs, and with how much monitoring is included. That is why the slider runs from $10k to $1M per ha.
